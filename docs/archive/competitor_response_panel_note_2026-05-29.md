Purpose:

```text
Compress three related strategic facts into one support panel:
1. which representative external objects occupy which release-stack layers,
2. whether each object should be fought, borrowed from, or defended against,
3. what that implies for where we should spend effort next.
```

## What the panel shows

### Panel A

Rows are representative external objects.
Columns are the `L1-L5` release-stack layers:

- `L1`: schema
- `L2`: partially observed benchmark
- `L3`: shared posterior-family object
- `L4`: mechanism-specific decoder / workflow layer
- `L5`: reporting / decision-support layer

The left action tags mark whether the correct response is:

- `FIGHT`
- `BORROW`
- `DEFEND`

This makes the main outside-in point visible at a glance:

- benchmark rivals matter most around `L2-L3`
- borrowing targets matter most around `L1/L4`
- platform threats matter most around `L5`

### Panel B

The lower action cards convert that occupancy view into response rules:

- `FIGHT`
  Benchmark neighbors such as `NC / Bannigan` and explainable LAI work
  should be met with stronger sparse-prefix OOD benchmark discipline and
  object-level framing.

- `BORROW`
  Data and workflow papers such as `Scientific Data`, AI-ready release
  infrastructure, and liposome IVR workflow are worth borrowing from because
  they improve schema discipline and mechanism-local assay packaging.

- `DEFEND`
  Platform-style systems such as `FormulationLAI`, `FormulationAI`, and
  broader optimization systems are not the best direct benchmark rivals, but
  they are the strongest pressure on the `decision-support` story.

## Why this panel matters

The project should not treat all external objects as one kind of competitor.
The correct response depends on the stack layer they pressure most.

That is the operational meaning of the current strategic position:

> occupy the shared release-intelligence stack, not just the single-model leaderboard.
